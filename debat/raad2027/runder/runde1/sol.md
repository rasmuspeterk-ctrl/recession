## A) Agenda decisions

### Q1 — λ and headline skill
**Keep λ = 1**, explicitly as an inherited protocol constant, not an independently validated optimum; amend §3 accordingly. This is acceptable despite knowing the results because continuity avoids another round of tuning, but neither the nine-block bootstrap nor its “within 2 SD” result establishes equivalence. Make the **all-live-publishable, oracle-free score the headline**: currently +20.37% over 219 origins, with fixed-λ +27.05%/198 and nested-λ +18.32% prominently labelled historical diagnostics. After the refit, recompute the applicable scores under the committed protocol; never present today’s figures as performance estimates for a changed configuration.

### Q2 — Model selection and gate
**Amend §4: curve-only is operational by council mandate; F5 is a frozen benchmark, not a candidate selected each origin.** This post-hoc amendment is acceptable as an explicit governance decision, not as evidence that historical selection would have found curve-only. Strike both proposed MDE-based interpretations: an MDE is an effect size detectable at specified power, **not a significance threshold**, and exceeding it does not establish evidence. Retain |Δskill| > 4.42 percentage points solely as the existing administrative agenda trigger; report paired loss differences, episode uncertainty and the gate’s approximately 21-point MDE, with no automatic promotion or demotion.

### Q3 — Frequency
**Adopt the monthly fit from September 2027 under the blueprint’s written full-monthly gate.** Both outcomes are known, but resolving a prose–implementation conflict in favour of the prior written specification is more defensible than inventing a status-quo tie-break after the prose passes. This is protocol compliance, not proof of monthly superiority: different origin sets and pooled baselines prevent that interpretation, and 591 overlapping windows are not independent observations. Seal the exact implementation now—monthly eligibility and standardisation, λ = 1, September refits plus approved event triggers—and label quarterly historical performance as belonging to the previous configuration.

### Q4 — Universe start
**Keep 1947Q2.** Retention after seeing the alternatives is acceptable because no demonstrated measurement failure requires exclusion, while moving the start materially changes the estimand and current probability. Permanently publish the three-start probability sensitivity and common-origin skill comparison, distinguishing the LTR splice from the changing recession mix. Do not describe +30.28% versus +27.05% as an improvement: their evaluation samples differ.

### Q5 — Demotion
**No operational change and no retrospective rebuilding of F5.** Correct the documentation: five features were *retained under an asymmetric incumbency rule*, not shown to pass symmetric admission. This post-hoc correction is acceptable because it withdraws an unsupported claim rather than changes predictions. Any future mandated search must specify a common inclusion rule for incumbents and challengers, an economic minimum effect, episode-level stability criteria and the complete search path before execution; “symmetric” alone is insufficient.

### Q6 — Content trigger
**Adopt September + hash + positive-label/relabel content triggers from the refit.** At each scheduled data check, construct the eligible labelled training records under the committed lag rule; trigger if a positive record is absent from the fitted manifest or an eligible record’s label has changed. Do not trigger merely because another negative origin matures, which would quietly replace annual refitting with rolling refitting. Adoption after observing E6 is acceptable because it removes arbitrary label-ingestion delays through a deterministic rule, not because its observed probability changes were small.

### Q7 — Ledger key
**Keep the immutable scoring key and reference, but withdraw any claim that its existing inferential machinery provides a validated significance test.** Publish the exact monthly replay alongside the original +20.4% reference, both logged-base-rate and pooled-base-rate scores, and all censoring variants; the logged baseline is a legitimate real-time comparator, not a constant-difficulty benchmark. This post-hoc disclosure is justified by a demonstrated calibration failure: simulated null rejection rates of 36–53% cannot be treated as nominal 5% inference, and three complete blocks are not sufficient evidence. Public wording should be: “Under this nine-block resampling design, estimated 80%-power requirements are roughly 150 calendar years and highly sensitive to cycle composition; the ledger primarily documents accountable forecasts.”

### Q8 — EBP/Baa
**Defer both tests; authorise data-quality work only.** The post-hoc decision is acceptable because it neither changes P nor manufactures an untouched holdout from already inspected onset histories. Archive monthly EBP vintages and measure historical revisions explicitly, for example \(r_{t,v}=EBP_t^{(v)}-EBP_t^{(v-1)}\), reporting maximum absolute revisions and whether candidate signal classifications change. Twelve snapshots are a minimum observation period, not proof of nonrevision; reopening still requires an economic rationale, council mandate, licensing/provenance resolution and a sealed test acknowledging contamination.

### Q9 — \(L_R\)
**Use peak announcements only for the onset-label availability audit.** Fix the definition now: \(L_R=\max(18,\lceil\max_j \ell_j^{peak}\rceil)\) months, using a committed peak-to-announcement lag calculation and applying changes prospectively under the existing eligibility convention. This result-aware clarification is acceptable because trough dates do not establish onset labels. Audit trough information separately for recession-state censoring; peak-only \(L_R\) does not validate that separate mechanism.

### Q10 — F29 ratification
**Ratify the trailing-month daily spread**, while recording that it is not measurement-identical to GS10−TB3MS and preserving the monthly-input comparator. **Ratify moving swarm tripwires out of ignition**, which restores the prior decision. **Ratify the logging schedule exactly as executable in the committed key**, not an ambiguous new paraphrase. **Ratify censoring inside an announced recession only as a public state/eligibility rule**: retain every scheduled ledger row, record the information available then, and never use a subsequently dated trough retrospectively; all four ratifications are post hoc procedural acceptance, not retrospective authorisation or evidence of improved accuracy.

## B) Verdict on Claude’s draft

- **D1 — Amend:** retain λ = 1; remove any suggestion that weak power proves CV unreliable. Lead with oracle-free performance and distinguish historical configurations.
- **D2 — Amend materially:** retain council-selected curve-only and frozen F5; strike “exceeds own MDE → evidence.”
- **D3 — Strike:** implement the written monthly gate. The monthly live reading does not settle the fit-frequency question.
- **D4 — Keep as written.**
- **D5 — Amend:** preserve F5 unchanged; specify an actual future inclusion procedure, not merely “symmetry.”
- **D6 — Amend:** define content precisely and exclude routine negative-label maturation.
- **D7 — Amend materially:** freeze scores, not invalid inferential claims; replace categorical practical-impossibility language with design-conditional power results.
- **D8 — Amend:** twelve EBP snapshots cannot certify an unrevisable history.
- **D9 — Amend:** separate onset-label lag auditing from trough-dependent censoring.
- **D10 — Amend:** ratify individually with the provenance and censoring qualifications above.

## C) Biggest risk in my proposal

**The monthly-fit switch may institutionalise a specification error disguised as textual fidelity.** Its historical gate comparison uses different samples and baselines, and monthly overlap changes effective weighting without adding independent cycles. I nevertheless prefer honouring the prior written gate to rewriting it after observing its result; this choice must not be advertised as demonstrated predictive improvement.
