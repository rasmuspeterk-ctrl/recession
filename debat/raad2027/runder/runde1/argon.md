In a univariate logit with $N=267$ and 8–9 effective episode blocks, inner cross-validation has catastrophic variance: picking $\lambda \in [30, 100]$ in early windows because of pre-1966 curve inversions is pure small-sample pathology, costing $-9.4$ pp in the 1970s–80s. While retaining $\lambda = 1$ after seeing E1 is post hoc, it is econometrically mandatory because estimating a hyperparameter on 6–8 blocks is statistically bankrupt. From 2027, the primary headline skill must be the **oracle-free supplement (+20.4 %)** alongside the **fixed-λ purged walk-forward (+27.1 %)**; demote nested-λ (+18.3 %) to a methodological footnote rather than co-headline, as printing noise as a co-headline confuses users.

**Q2 (model selection and the gate).** Formally amend Blueprint §4 to abolish per-origin model selection; curve-only is locked by council mandate. E1 proves that inner-CV model selection is anti-predictive ($\rho = -0.16$ between inner-CV advantage and realised advantage) and driven 75.7 % by a single 6-quarter post-WWII inflation block (1947Q2–1948Q3). From 2027, the F5 benchmark gate is diagnostic only and never triggers an automatic switch. Drop the borrowed 4.42 pp threshold entirely—using a frequency-test MDE for a feature-set comparison is a category error. The sole gate threshold is the gate’s own episode-block MDE ($\approx 21.06$ pp): if $\text{Skill}(F5) - \text{Skill}(\text{curve}) > \text{MDE}_{\text{gate}}$ at a September refit, it triggers a council review, nothing more.

**Q3 (frequency).** Keep the quarterly fit from 2027. The literal reading of Blueprint §2 (+29.24 % full monthly vs +27.05 % quarterly) compares apples to oranges: 591 overlapping 12-month horizons have different pooled base rates and massive serial correlation, not 591 independent trials. The code’s quarter-thinned reading ($m \pmod 3 == 0$ at +26.03 %) failed the 26.05 % bar by 0.02 pp solely due to phase noise (the other two phases score +32.13 % and +29.58 %). Because both outcomes are known, switching to a monthly fit now to capture a steeper slope ($\beta = -1.41$ vs $-1.29$) is unjustifiable post-hoc tuning, especially since the live input already uses the trailing month of daily DGS10 − DTB3.

**Q4 (universe start).** Keep 1947Q2 as the locked universe start. Truncating to 1953Q2 or 1960Q1 is tempting because pre-1953 relies on Shiller's spliced LTR and pre-1960 has a 36.6 % base rate (reflecting short post-war inventory cycles). However, E3 shows that moving the start date shifts live $P(+1.12)$ from 14.9 % down to 12.4 % or 10.4 %, largely by lowering the intercept via the fit base rate (18.0 % $\to$ 14.6 %). Changing the sample start after seeing that it lowers live $P$ by up to 4.5 pp violates our core anti-p-hacking ground rule. Print the three-start sensitivity vector $[P_{1947}, P_{1953}, P_{1960}]$ permanently in the refit manifest.

**Q5 (demotion).** Document E4 as the post-mortem on why F5 failed (+23.16 % vs +27.05 %): four of its five features were only retained via an asymmetric incumbency bias (+1/+1 pp drop bar vs +2/+2 pp add bar). Keep F5 frozen as the historical 5-feature benchmark so the ledger’s benchmark column stays continuous, but Mandate that any future feature search must apply a symmetric zero-incumbency bar: every feature $j$ in a candidate set $S$ must satisfy $\Delta \text{LL}(S \setminus \{j\} \to S) \ge +2.0\text{ pp}$, $\Delta \text{Brier} \ge +2.0\text{ pp}$, and $\ge 80\%$ LOEO folds positive.

**Q6 (content trigger).** Adopt the content trigger effective at the September-2027 refit. The current rule (September + NBER table hash change) is flawed: when NBER announces a peak, the hash changes, but because of the 18-month publication lag floor ($L_R = 18$), the new onset's positive quarters have not yet matured into `eligible_fit` (0 positives added in 6/6 historical peak announcements). Waiting until the next September or a happenstance trough announcement introduces a random 0–9 month delay (85 origin-months historically). Define the trigger mechanically: at each monthly run at date $t$, construct `eligible_fit(t)` using $t - L_R$; if `hash(eligible_fit(t).labels) != manifest.eligible_fit_hash`, execute a refit.

**Q9 (L_R).** **All announcements (raising $L_R$ to 21 months)**—I strongly oppose Claude's D9 here. In the live engine and E5, origins *inside* an ongoing recession are censored, and the end of censoring is determined by the NBER **trough** announcement. If a trough is announced with a 21-month lag (as in 1991 and 2001), the censoring boundary for `eligible_fit` is not known in real time until month 21; claiming $L_R = 18$ pretends we knew the recession had ended at month 18 and could score post-trough quarters as eligible label-0 origins. Set $L_R = \max_{c} (\text{lag}_{\text{peak}}(c), \text{lag}_{\text{trough}}(c)) = 21$ months before the audit runs.

### C) Single Biggest Risk in My Proposal

**Changing $L_R$ from 18 to 21 months (Q9/D9) alters the `eligible_fit` boundary at the September-2027 refit by delaying the entry of the most recent quarter by 3 months.** If we are in a normal expansion in September 2027, $L_R = 21$ means the fit window closes at 2024Q4 ($N=270$) instead of 2025Q1 ($N=271$), slightly shifting the refit weights from the published F35 prediction ($N=271$). Furthermore, if a recession onset actually occurred in 2026, raising $L_R$ to 21 delays the content-triggered entry of that new positive label by 3 months (costing ~0.4 pp of recalibration responsiveness, as shown in E6). However, accepting a 3-month delay is a necessary price to eliminate look-ahead bias in post-trough censoring.
