**Claude Opus 5.5 — Round 1 (interested party: I ran E1–E6 and wrote D1–D10)**

**A) Answers**

**Q1 (λ).** Keep λ = 1 fixed and amend §3 to say so. Nested λ is not "the honest version" of the same procedure;
it is a different procedure whose λ estimate is dominated by which 9–13 episode blocks happen to be in the
window: early windows choose 30–100 because the curve had negative inner skill before 1966, and the λ chosen
moves with the inner-CV design (expanding designs pick 0.1). The bootstrap puts nested − fixed at −8.7 pp,
SD 5.5. Headline from 2027: the triple "fixed-λ walk-forward / nested-λ walk-forward / oracle-free
supplement", never one number alone. I concede λ = 1 is not cleanly a priori (inherited from v4, which was
built on overlapping history); that is why the nested number must be printed beside it permanently.

**Q2 (selection, gate).** Amend §4: curve-only is operational by council decision (V501 §6), not by per-origin
selection, and no selector is run in operation. E1 shows every pre-registrable selector loses 9–10 pp, all of
it in 2022–24, because F5's inner-CV advantage is 72–76 % one six-row block from 1947–48. The gate becomes
two-tier and switches nothing: |Δ| > 4.42 pp → mandatory item on the next council agenda; |Δ| > the gate's own
MDE (bootstrap 1.96·SD of the paired difference, today 21.06 pp) → may be described as evidence.

**Q3 (frequency).** Quarterly. Both readings are known, so either choice is post hoc and the status quo wins the
tie. §2's purpose was timeliness, and the live input is already a monthly trailing-month reading; a monthly
fit only steepens β from −1.29 to −1.41 (−0.5 pp in P today). The code reading's failure is phase-dependent
(only m%3==0 fails, by 0.02 pp), so neither reading is a robust test. Record G1.3 as "readings disagree,
status quo retained", with both published.

**Q4 (start).** Keep 1947Q2. Moving it now is chosen knowing it lowers P 2.5–4.5 pp; the verdict word, the
walk-forward and the gate direction are unchanged at every start. Print P at 1953Q2/1960Q1 permanently.

**Q5 (demotion).** Nothing in operation. Documentation: "5 incumbents pass" holds only under the asymmetric rule;
under the incumbent-as-challenger rule only the curve survives; under drop-symmetry nobody falls. If a search is
ever mandated again, the sealed spec fixes ONE symmetric bar (add-effect ≥ +2/+2 pp and ≥ 7/8 folds at level,
for incumbents and challengers alike) before any run.

**Q6 (content trigger).** Adopt from the 2027 refit: refit in any month where `eligible_fit(R)` contains a
positive label or relabel absent from the manifest in operation, in addition to September and the hash
trigger. It is mechanical and removes a 0–9-month lag whose size depends on when NBER announces troughs.
Cost: ~18 extra refits per 49 years, |ΔP| ≤ 1.05 pp historically.

**Q7 (key).** No amendment: the key was preregistered before row 4 and amending it after E5 is exactly the
freedom it exists to remove. Publish E5 as a supplement with real-time (+24.4 %), real-time without the
USREC-vintage assumption (+29.0 %) and oracle (+33.3 %) side by side, plus the fact that rule (a)'s logged
base rate averages 22.8 % against a realised 15.3 %. Public statement: "The ledger cannot reach a verdict at
80 % power within any practical horizon (≈ 150 calendar years with the 2020–24 block in the pool). It is an
honesty instrument, not a test."

**Q8 (EBP/Baa).** Defer. Contamination is declared but not curable; EBP revision behaviour is unaudited; Baa is
not redistributable, so a Baa feature would make P non-reproducible from the public repo; with MDE ≈ 4.4 pp
on 8 onsets a realistic gain is undetectable. Re-examine only after ≥ 12 monthly hashed EBP snapshots show
whether its history revises.

**Q9 (L_R).** Peak announcements measured from the peak month. The label is the onset; trough announcements
end censoring but never decide an onset label, so they do not belong in the onset-lag audit.

**Q10 (F29).** Ratify all four individually. The daily trailing-month curve is the same statistic without
publication lag (tested); each row since row 4 carries P on the monthly figure, so the instrument change is
auditable. The other three restore an earlier decision or were preregistered in the key.

**B) Verdict on my own draft.** I keep D1–D10. The weakest are D3 and D6. D3 relies on "status quo wins ties",
and the literal §2 passes in all six cells, so a fair reader could call D3 a refusal to apply our own
blueprint. D6 changes refit timing, so it changes P, by small amounts but in exactly the months that matter
(after an onset).

**C) Biggest risk.** D1 and D2 together lock in the choice that looks best on the full path (fixed λ = 1,
curve-only) and justify it by distrusting every procedure that would have chosen differently. If the 2022–24
false alarm is the new regime rather than an outlier, that is precisely the wrong lock, and the only safeguard
is that the nested numbers stay printed beside the headline.
