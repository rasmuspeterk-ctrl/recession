**A) Agenda**

**Q1 (λ).** Keep λ = 1 fixed and amend §3. The reason is not that fixed beats nested by 9 pp (−8.7 ± 5.5 by block bootstrap is not evidence of anything); it is that the inner CV is structurally unable to choose λ: with 9–13 episode blocks it picks λ 30–100 in every pre-1990 window, i.e. it selects a near-degenerate model, and the 11 designs span +18.0…+23.4 %. A one-covariate standardised logit on n ≥ 267 is barely sensitive to λ anyway (P 15.1 / 15.8 / 18.2 at λ = 1 / 3 / 30). Post hoc acceptability: yes — λ = 1 is the incumbent, the preregistered interval for nested held, and the only new information is that selection is unreliable. Headline from 2027: **the oracle-free fixed-λ figure (+20.4 %, refit ≈ +17 %)**, because it is the only number a live reader could have obtained; +27.1 and nested +18.3 are printed beside it as "full-origin" and "selector sensitivity". Opus's D1 keeps +27.1 as headline; I don't — it uses labels unknown at the origin.

**Q2 (selection, gate).** Amend §4: no per-origin selector is run; curve-only is operational by council decision. The evidence is unambiguous: the inner-CV advantage of F5 has r = −0.16 with its realised advantage, 76 % of it comes from six 1947–48 rows, and every selector loses 9–10 pp. Gate from 2027: two tiers as in D2, but computed on the *common* origin set, and F5's start-sensitivity (E3: +23.2 / −0.7 / +3.7 %) must be printed in the same line — a benchmark whose sign flips when 24 pre-GS10 quarters are removed is not a benchmark, it is a sensitivity plot. The 4.42 pp tier is an agenda trigger only; the 21 pp tier is the only one that may be called "evidence".

**Q3 (frequency).** Switch to the monthly fit. The preregistered object is the blueprint text; the code thinning was an implementation error, and the general rule "spec governs code when they conflict" is outcome-independent and must be written down as such. The literal reading passes in 6/6 cells, the code reading fails by 0.02 pp in the one phase that happens to coincide with quarterly origins. The monthly fit also matches the live input (a trailing-month reading fitted to monthly history, not to quarter-end snapshots), and the P difference today is 0.5 pp. Conditions: episode-block bootstrap stays the band; the 591 overlapping windows are never described as 591 observations; skill is reported on both origin grids.

**Q4 (start).** Keep 1947Q2. It is a locked constant; moving it is a choice made knowing it lowers P by 2.5–4.5 pp, in the direction the current readings already point. The β is actually steeper without the Shiller years (−1.41 vs −1.29), so the issue is base rate, not slope. Add a permanent supplementary column: P at 1953Q2 and 1960Q1 starts in every ledger row, unscored.

**Q5 (demotion).** Nothing changes in operation. Document that "5 incumbents pass" is an artefact of the asymmetric rule and that under incumbent-as-challenger both simultaneous and backward elimination end at curve-only — read jointly with E3 and E1, this is the strongest evidence that realrate/dd/d_infl/cape_pct are noise. Any future search: one bar in both directions, ≥ +2/+2 pp, ≥ 7/8 LOEO folds, sign stable, fixed in the sealed spec. cape_pct (+4.11/+2.37, 5/8 folds) is explicitly recorded as "not demoted, not confirmed".

**Q6 (content trigger).** Reject. Mean |ΔP| 0.13 pp, max 1.05 pp, below the 1-decimal rounding of P in 90 % of months; against that, 18 data-dependent refits per 49 years, each a new manifest and a discontinuity the ledger key's replay (E5) does not model (it assumes September refits with frozen rounded weights). A preregistered schedule should not become label-dependent for a tenth of a percentage point. Record the 0–9-month lag as a known, bounded property.

**Q7 (ledger key).** Do not amend the scored key. Publish E5 as a supplement with all three censoring variants, the logged-vs-realised base rate (22.8 % vs 15.3 %) and pooled ex post +21.2 %. Public statement, verbatim: "The ledger requires ≈130 years of scored rows (≈150 calendar years) to distinguish the model from the base rate at 80 % power under realistic clustering; naive tests that claim significance after 5–20 years reject a true null 36–53 % of the time. The ledger exists to make each forecast auditable, not to validate the model." Additive change only (no scored row touched): see Q10 item 4.

**Q8 (EBP/Baa).** Close Baa now — a non-redistributable local file is incompatible with a reproducible protocol, full stop. Defer EBP with a hard condition: ≥ 12 monthly snapshots with |revision| < 0.05 at all lags, after which a sealed spec must precommit sign, magnitude (≥ +4.4 pp on common origins) and the admission that the 1990/2001 values have been seen, so only post-2027 behaviour counts as a test. With ~3 usable onsets, expect the answer to be "never admitted"; say so in advance.

**Q9 (L_R).** All announcements, L_R = 21. D9's logic is wrong: the label is the onset, but *censoring* is determined by whether the origin lies between peak and trough. In 1980Q4–1981Q1 an origin is "censored" or "label 0/1" depending on a trough announced 20–21 months later. Eligibility must guarantee that label *and* censoring status are final at R; peak-only does not. Cost: three fewer eligible quarters — acceptable.

**Q10 (F29).** Ratify individually: (1) trailing-month daily DGS10 − DTB3 — yes, provided the monthly GS10 − TB3MS P stays logged in every row; (2) tripwires out of ignition — yes, it restores R3; (3) log day — yes, already in the key; (4) live censoring inside an announced recession — ratify *with* an unscored supplementary column recording P and outcome for censored months. E5 shows this exact rule removes the entire 1980–81 run-up and costs 4.5 pp of replay skill; the double-dip is the case where the live model matters most, and discarding it silently is the one F29 change that was not merely operational.

**C) Biggest risk in my proposal**

Q3. I invoke "spec governs code" after seeing that the spec reading passes and the code reading fails. Had the signs been reversed I would like to believe I would still argue spec governs code — but Rasmus should not take my word for it, and neither should I. The honest mitigation is to adopt the precedence rule *as a general protocol clause* applying to every future spec/code conflict regardless of direction, and to publish the quarterly walk-forward in parallel for the life of the model so that the 2.19 pp (CI excluding zero only on overlapping windows) can be seen to persist or evaporate. If the council finds that rule too convenient, I would rather keep quarterly than adopt monthly on a selective principle.
