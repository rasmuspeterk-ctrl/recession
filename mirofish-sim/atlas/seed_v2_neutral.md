# US Macro & Market Situation Report — August 2026


## 1. The headline configuration: "low spark, high fuel"

The US economy shows few classic recession triggers (low spark), while market valuation and leverage are at historic extremes (high fuel). The tension between a resilient real economy and an extremely priced market is the central uncertainty of the next 12 months.

## 2. Real economy (the "spark" side — currently quiet)

- Real GDP growth: +2.1% (Q1 2026) and +1.5% (Q2 2026), annualized. Two positive prints; no technical recession signal.
- Unemployment: 4.1% (July 2026). Sahm rule real-time indicator: −0.03 — no labor-market deterioration signal (trigger is +0.50).
- Initial jobless claims: ~199,000/week (early August 2026) — historically low.
- Building permits: 1.374M (June 2026), softening but not collapsing.
- CPI inflation: 3.5% y/y (June 2026), re-accelerating from 2.7% a year earlier (+0.86pp change). Headline PCE ≈ 3.7% y/y.
- Fed funds (effective): 3.63%. Real cash yield vs PCE: −0.04pp — cash now yields slightly negative in real terms ("debasement" flag active).
- Yield curve (10y − 3m): +0.87pp, positive. The 2022–2024 inversion (deepest −1.57pp) ended November 2024, 20 months ago. Historically, the dangerous aftermath window after long inversions has largely passed without recession.

## 3. Markets (the "fuel" side — extreme)

- S&P 500: at its 12-month high (0% drawdown).
- Shiller CAPE: 42.0 — around the 93rd–99th percentile of all observations since 1881. Comparable levels: 1929, 1999–2000, 2021.
- Equity risk premium (1/CAPE − 10y yield): −2.22pp — NEGATIVE. Stocks are priced to yield less than Treasuries on a cyclically adjusted basis, as in 1999–2000.
- Margin debt: +51.5% y/y — a speculative-leverage expansion comparable to 1999 and 2007 episodes.
- High-yield credit spreads (HY OAS): 271bp — near cycle tights; credit markets price almost no stress (recession warning trigger is 600bp).
- Pattern summary: 3 of 3 "repricing" flags active (valuation, negative ERP, margin debt); 0 of 4 recession flags active; 1 of 1 debasement flag active.

## 4. Key actors in this economy

- The Federal Reserve: holding at 3.63% with inflation re-accelerating above target; faces pressure both to cut (growth protection, political pressure) and to hold/hike (inflation at 3.5% and rising).
- US households: employment strong, but real cash returns just turned negative; consumer spending has held up.
- Retail investors: fully invested, record margin leverage, buying every dip; sentiment euphoric.
- Institutional investors / hedge funds: aware of extreme valuations, positioned momentum-long, watching credit spreads and labor data for the turn.
- Corporations: refinancing needs into 2027 at higher real rates; earnings currently supporting prices.
- Homebuilders / real estate: permits softening under still-elevated long rates.
- Commentators / media: split between "soft landing achieved, new bull market" and "1999/2007-style bubble top" narratives.

## 5. The forecasting question and known risks

Question: Does the US economy enter a recession within the next 12 months (by August 2027)?

Transmission risks to watch: (a) inflation re-acceleration forces renewed tightening → curve re-inverts, (b) an equity/credit repricing from extreme valuations and leverage tightens financial conditions enough to hit the real economy (reverse wealth effect, margin spirals), (c) labor market cracks appear (Sahm > 0.5, claims trend up), (d) an exogenous shock (unforecastable by construction).

Historical anchors: extreme CAPE + negative ERP + margin booms (1929, 2000, 2007, 2021) preceded major market repricings; but market repricing does not always produce recession (1987, 2022). Positive, re-steepened curve after a completed inversion cycle has usually meant the recession either already happened — or the signal failed (2023 false alarm).
